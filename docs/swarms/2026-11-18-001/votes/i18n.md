# i18n vote — C (14 locales + catalogue-count test)

## Rationale (i18n / RTL)

`apps/web/lib/i18n.ts` ships only three catalogues — `en`, `fr`, `he` — and the source itself labels `he` "the RTL proof, not as a shipped translation" (line 19). REQ-057 (`spec/01-requirements.md:465`) promises "14 locales ship" and RTL for Hebrew and Arabic-class locales, with the rationale "declared support must be real." Today support is only declared: `t()` falls back to English and `direction()` already derives `rtl` for `ar/fa/he/ur` including region subtags, but catalogues for the other 11 locales do not exist. The two expensive-to-retrofit rules are already pinned by the REQ-057 tests in `shell.spec.ts`; the missing, high-leverage gate is exactly the catalogue-count/key-parity test. C proves the pipeline and locks the count *before* the copy-heavy slices land. A and B do not advance i18n correctness — they consume it.

Two things C surfaces that A/B would hide: the spec conflict (14 in REQ-057/issue #16 vs 15 in `04-nonfunctional.md:113`, TRACE and ADR-014) and the still-open DEC-11 ("Locales to ship"). A count test forces that decision into the open instead of letting support drift silently.

## Cost of new copy across three catalogues

A (approval card, REQ-007/017) and B (board/triage, REQ-036/038) add many new user-visible keys: pending-card states, approve/deny/receipt actions, board columns, ticket and triage states. ADR-014 forbids hardcoded English and requires every key used in the UI to exist in the catalogue, so each new key must be authored in all three catalogues to avoid falling back to English, plus RTL mirroring checks for `he`. Under C that surface multiplies by ~5× (14 locales). Sequencing C first fixes the count and the key-parity rule once, so A/B copy is written against a proven pipeline rather than a 3-locale placeholder that must be redone. Writing A or B first means writing their copy three times now and fourteen times later, with no guard against drift.

## First REQ-named test

`test("REQ-057 all 14 catalogues ship, share the en key set, and mirror RTL", ...)` in `apps/web/tests/shell.spec.ts`: assert `SUPPORTED_LOCALES.length === 14`, that every catalogue's key set equals the `en` key set, and that `direction("he")`/`direction("ar-EG")` are `rtl`. This encodes the locale decision as a machine-checked contract and blocks any future silent catalogue.
